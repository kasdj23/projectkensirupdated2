Our story

1893

Origins and Early Development

Pepsi was created in 1893 by Caleb Bradham, a pharmacist in New Bern, North Carolina, who first called it “Brad’s Drink”. Bradham formulated it to aid digestion and boost energy, using ingredients like sugar, vanilla, and kola nuts. In 1898, he renamed it Pepsi-Cola, referencing dyspepsia (indigestion) and the supposed digestive benefits of the drink, although it never contained pepsin. Bradham incorporated the Pepsi-Cola Company in 1902, and by 1904, sales had reached nearly 20,000 gallons, aided by early celebrity endorsements such as racing legend Barney Oldfield.

1923

Challenges and Revival

Despite early success, Pepsi faced financial instability due to fluctuating sugar prices and the aftermath of World War I, leading Bradham to declare bankruptcy in 1923. The brand was purchased by Charles Guth, president of Loft Candy Company, who reformulated the syrup and leveraged his candy store network to expand distribution. Guth introduced the 12-ounce bottle during the Great Depression, emphasizing value with the “Nickel, Nickel” jingle, which doubled sales and helped Pepsi gain a foothold against Coca-Cola.

1940s

Marketing and Cultural Impact

Pepsi became known for innovative marketing, including targeting the African American market in the 1940s with positive portrayals and endorsements, boosting its market share. Iconic slogans like “Pepsi-Cola Hits The Spot” and celebrity endorsements from figures such as Joan Crawford helped cement its cultural presence. Pepsi also engaged in the famous “cola wars” with Coca-Cola, including the Pepsi Challenge taste tests and responses to New Coke.

1965

Global Expansion and Corporate Growth

Pepsi expanded internationally, including a landmark barter deal with the Soviet Union, and continued to grow through acquisitions. In 1965, the Pepsi-Cola Company merged with Frito-Lay, Inc., forming PepsiCo, which later acquired Tropicana (1998) and Quaker Oats (2001), adding brands like Gatorade to its portfolio. Today, PepsiCo operates in over 200 countries, serving more than a billion customers daily, and remains a major player in the global food and beverage industry.
